## Tables, figures, methods, and claims

- The v4 aggregate table is sourced from saved-v4 recomputation, not a new run. Macro OvR ROC-AUC and unweighted macro mean OvR AP are defined in the methods. The same five-class convention is used throughout.
- The sequence partition table is from the common 5,842-row paired cohort, not from all target-bearing district-years. The draft states that distinction.
- The per-class table and confusion matrix describe the validation-selected enhanced v4 LSTM only. It is clearly separate from five-seed model averages.
- `five_seed_macro_f1.png` uses saved five-seed mean and across-seed SD. `five_seed_ablation_macro_f1.png` uses paired same-seed differences and their SD. `selected_v4_enhanced_lstm_confusion_matrix.png` uses the recomputed saved-v4 confusion matrix. All are generated by `ML_Implementation/plot_final_paper_figures.py`; no model is fitted by this script.
- The target is consistently described as ordered burden classes and explicitly as a project-defined adaptation. The random-split notebook is excluded from future-forecast claims. The draft does not claim that structural t−1 availability proves data was published before a real forecast date.
- Missing target rows, partially observed grouped sums, missing historical district-years, and nonfinite feature histories are distinguished. Observed zero is not recoded as missing or vice versa.
- The source formulas, `min_count=1` behavior, no additional stored-group transformation, direct overlap list, duplicate disposition, and unresolved candidate schema mappings are described or linked to evidence.
- No result is described as causal importance or statistically significant. Dependence-aware population uncertainty was not estimated.

## Reference check

1. Mao et al. (2025): title, six authors, journal, volume/issue/pages and DOI match the publisher page. The manuscript uses it only for related-work context about city-partition crime-count sequence forecasting and binning; it does not attribute the NCRB target construction to that paper. [Publisher record](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14727978251337993).
2. Hochreiter and Schmidhuber (1997): authors, title, *Neural Computation* 9(8), 1735–1780 and DOI match the MIT Press bibliographic record. [MIT Press record](https://direct.mit.edu/neco/article/9/8/1735/6109/Long-Short-Term-Memory).
3. Government of India OGD catalog: title and NCRB attribution are consistent with the linked record. It supports source catalog attribution, not every local transformation or original-source provenance. [OGD record](https://www.data.gov.in/catalog/district-wise-crimes-under-various-sections-indian-penal-code-ipc-crimes).
4. scikit-learn `f1_score`: linked developer documentation supports the named metric; the paper defines its own multiclass macro convention in the methods. [Documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.metrics.f1_score.html).
5. India Code IPC reference is only used for legal definitions and explicitly not as evidence of NCRB's statistical counting or crosswalk rules. [IPC Act text](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/11091/1/the_indian_penal_code%2C_1860.pdf).

These checks verify bibliographic metadata and whether the paper's narrow use is supported; they are not a comprehensive literature review.
